Hats off to Alessandro, who released a revised edition of his Spectrumpedia this year. The first edition was published in Italian in 2012. It is now spread over 2 volumes, covering more subjects and in greater depth. It's a Hitchiker's Guide for anything you could wish to learn about any Spectrum hardware (official or unofficial), utilities, software, and the past, present and future of the scene. There are now Italian, English and Spanish versions, and fingers crossed a publisher shows some interest because this is a landmark title that will have customers.

Woot would like to acknowledge Andre Leao's / Planeta Sinclair's huge contribution to Speccy preservation. It would be fair to call him the driving force these days. The SC forum's preservation section sees at least one uMIA or MIA announced daily. There is a huge variety of software being discovered, such as The Powerbox train sims, Bell ringing software, educational titles, domestic and building software, aircraft design, morse code tutors, bird databases plus more games than there is memory in this Spectrum to list.

Ah yes, Yokai - the downright disturbing creatures of Japanese myth, first introduced to many of us through Alessandro's 2013 game "Funky Fungus".In this game, Yokai have invaded and overpowered the old monastery. You are Sheng-Li, a talented young monk. Your strength is the Wandering Spirit technique, and you must enter the soul of each Yokai and control them into an 'assisted suicide'. When each Yokai is dispatched, you may re-enter your physical body and go to the exit.This makes for a great action/puzzle game, and being by Ariel Endaraues, it's top quality. Like his other games, he manages to find an original way of using AGD and turning it into a very polished game.The levels are really well designed, and a lot of thought has gone into easing the player in. It wisely has infinite lives, as there is little sense in starting over (though there is a quit option), and these days we need to encourage the modern player.
This is one of my top picks of the year. Give it a go - and stay away from the walkthrough vids for as long as you can!

This is an updated version of the teaser demo that was released in 2018. It has more gameplay, snazzy AY and an end of game boss. It was very well received, and rightly so because it's a good game. However, it did leave me wondering what the author got so right, to attract the attention of more jaded old gamers than is typical for a platform game. I think it's because it's short, easy, very stylish and immersive. Someone else also suggested to me that it's basically the best game never made for the TRS-80!This extended version is basically only the size of what we would once have called a demo. I suppose it's good to leave the punters wanting more, but if Dom ever decides to make a sequel, then it could be twice or thrice the size without losing the audience.

Loxley is built on a BASIC game Ariel Ruiz made in 1991, back when the Soviet Union collapsed, Bohemian
Rhapsody topped the charts and the Speccy's commercial era ended (these events may or may not be linked). It is also based on the Kevin Costner film released in the summer of that year.
Firstly, we are treated to an intro, with full screen animated graphics, digitised speech, and an explanation of the plot - all you need to know is that Alan Rickman has kidnapped Maid Marian and you've got to rescue her. Thankfully the AY music is a pleasant ditty, and not a rendition of that bloody Bryan Adams song that was drilled into our heads for so long BITD.The large masked sprites moving against a detailed background are worthy of a top-end commercial era release - imagine Robocop set in medieval Nottinghamshire.

The sword fights present enough of a challenge on easy - you need to block high, middle and low based on your opponent's stance, at a speed that does sometimes beat my ageing reflexes over the course of a game. Thank heavens Robin has an inexhaustible supply of arrows, as using the bow is fun, and the arrows give a satisfying thud before leaving a shaft stuck in solid scenery.The more you fight and train, the stronger your bow and swordmanship become - something you will need later in the game. You need to steal money, which can be used to buy health potions and shortcuts to fighting power, but some of it must be reserved for the adventure element in the game - give to the poor and all that.
Some locations are interactive puzzles, where you need to solve a rune or encourage a rat (by shooting at it) towards a lever. I love the style of the game map, and its instant, penalty free travel without restrictions.

To sum up, the game is a mix of action, platform, adventure, management and puzzle - basically there's a lot to it. I was less keen on jumping over patrolling snakes or flying creatures - that temporarily demoted the game to bog standard platforming, but at least it was rare.I was determined not to use the phrase 'great for compiled BASIC' in this review, and I'm pleased to say I didn't even need to try - it is just great.This game has similarities to Wudang, another one of Ariel's triumphs, but I found Loxley more interesting, better to play and easier to get into. This game deserves a proper shot - play it on easy and give it a go.128 times better than the film.

It's great to see authors like Simon, who started releasing Speccy games in the mid 1980's and never stopped. Previously he used QUILL, presumably on his real Speccy, and now it's PAW on his Spectrum NEXT.The presentation and loading screen are pure oldschool, but the plot is contemporary. Escape rooms, in case you don't know, became popular in the 2010's and they involve being locked in a room and solving puzzles to get out. You also pay for the privilege.On paper, Locked In! is the perfect game for the fairweather adventurer: it's a linear 11 screen escape room text adventure, where objects cannot be transferred between rooms. Some of these puzzles are brilliant, involving creative thinking, and perhaps a little googling. Because each room is a self-contained puzzle, you know that everything you need is at hand and you don't need to worry about missing something 10 screens back. Schoolboy knowledge of calculators is also a benefit.

Unfortunately, some bits of the game are impossible to all but the most battle-addled adventurer. One part even required detailed knowledge of the PAW input system. Once you encounter such features, you can never quite trust that the next puzzle will be fair.Despite my criticism, this is a great adventure - as long as you know the following:* STAND ON SHELF (don't worry it'll make sense later)
* keep track of the colour doors you pass through
* you need to input the 10 door colours at the end, but PAW can't handle 10 letter input, so you have to SAY it as 2 x 5 letter strings with a space between.I don't consider these tips as spoilers - they are bugfixes :-)
