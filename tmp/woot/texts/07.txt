Most outlets will be declaring this year's best game on all formats to be Baldur's Gate 3, a fantasy RPG with a party of four on a quest, fighting through turn-based tactical fantasy battles to win the day. Meanwhile, we have Wycheweald, which feels like that game's 8-bit ancestor. Same thing, but 8-bittier.Wycheweald starts out in the middle of a catastrophe - outsiders have sacked your village, and your neighbours are scattered. Who the invaders are, and why they've shown up, is revealed over the course of the game as you start solving quests, adding members to your party and slowly increasing your tactical options.With your explorations presented in a first-person window, the core combat has you positioning your team in a 2x2 grid, while your enemies do the same, limiting who can hit who, who can defend their team and so on.

Managing your attack and defence each turn, and rationing the scarce action-points used for special moves & spells is where the gameplay is. It starts out easy, perhaps a little too easy, but ramps up the difficulty as the story progresses. Weapons
and armour are a big part of dealing with this ramp-up, as is traditional, and naturally your characters will level up and get additional options.Nothing ground-breaking then, but with clear graphics, atmospheric music, enough story to keep you interested and enough tactical options to keep you challenged, it all hangs together beautifully.Even back in the day, the Spectrum had few games of this genre and now that BG3 has made RPGs cool again, it's fitting that our little rainbow menace has its own top-notch example for 2023.

Mid August saw 3 games sold on itch.io by a new group called RetroTeam. The first was "Sword of Destiny", an oldschool platforms and ladders game that perfectly captured the oldschool mid 80's feel. The second was "Cosmic Lander", an unforgiving inertia game that was redolent of post commercial era homebrew. Then "Zetrix" followed, a tetris game similar to so many 1990s Russian clones.And strangley enough, it soon transpired that that was EXACTLY what they were! Sword of Destiny was a cracked version of a 1985 Croatian game called "Cudesni Mac", Cosmic Lander was a crack of a 1993 Russian game called "Soft Landing" and Zetrix was the 1995 Belarusian "Ammytris". The only changes were loading screen, font and translated text...At least BITD, Harry S Price put in a bit of effort!

Thanks to the eagled-eyed people on the SC forum that spotted this scam, and subsequently reported it to itch.io, the Retroteam page is now a 404 error. Hopefully we don't see more of this. The most worrying part is that it's likely to have been done by a member of the community.If any of the issues in this article affect you, like the suffering of losing money to that crook, then please contact the Woot counselling service (I've got to recoup that fiver somehow!)

JANKY JOE IN RETRO HELL

Sloanysoft, a father/son team, are known for quirky MPAGD platform games where much of the fun comes from exploring the numerous retro references scattered throughout.Janky Joe is the main protagonist (and one of the weirdest sprites you'll ever see), but you also play as Hunchback, Cybernoid, a hat (!), the 404 Dinosaur and Bomb Jack to name a few. You get 13 lives, which may sound a lot, but even with a bit of training you'll probably need at least that.This is a game to sit back, enjoy some good honest arcade fun and eagerly await which retro-riff the next level has.This game gives a warming nostalgia glow - perfect for Christmas.It's time to GET JANKY!

This is no cookie cutter MPAGD game. ExoTical uses a lot of features of the engine, and a lot of bespoke sprite behaviours beyond the defaults.It is 2533 and Earth-3 is dying. Your task is to take the inexhaustible energy source from the exoplanet to save your homeland. I do wonder how people choose the year for their barmy futuristic backstories.The game is a flip screen shooter, with some nods to Cybernoid, but it's by no means a clone. The most original touches are the use of the unstable Xenodactyl egg as a weapon - you can carry and drop it as an explosive charge. And we have end of level bosses, which are okay, but no matter how huge they are, when they are mainly made of static blocks plus a few sprites, it's hard to feel you're fighting one entity. Still, Roolandoo did a great job with what AGD had to offer.The game is hard, and I doubt anyone other than the author will play it to completion without cheating. I'd say go for snapshots and enjoy the whole game - it's worth seeing, and the ending has a message mayyyd.

QUEST FOR THE CROWN
FRANK BIRTWISTLE STARTED 1986, released 2023. s rare that rediscovered software is this good. Quest for the Crown is the result of an impressively productive summer holiday in 1986. When most of us were lounging around watching "Why Dont You" and eating Quavers, Frank Birtwistle was beavering away with G.A.C in the back bedroom. A couple of copies of the finished adventure game were sent to software companies, but there were no replies and that was the end of that.
Fast forward 37 years of attic storage, Frank saved the tapes from the bin, and after a bit of trial and error, recovered the game as a .wav and shared it online.
Quest for the Crown is a treat to play. The pictures radiate old school charm, and in full of colour despite being created on a B&W telly. Its a challenging adventure, which flies in the face of text adventure tradition by having mostly fair puzzles. I love that the first action is "GET LAMP". Everything about it is (naturally) of its time, and god dont we love it for that.
