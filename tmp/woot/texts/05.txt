IS AN INTERFACE TWOOOOOOO

It does annoy me when another coder's first game for the Speccy is this good.
Escape from Monjas is an adventure game set on a space station, and your goal is to get off it and live a better life. The plot alone betrays that it's not your regular 'blow it up and escape with the money' adventure - Monjas is riddled with whimsy. It's funny, and not just funny for a game - it's properly funny.I love the author's distinctive style. It's rare to see large cartoony graphics using lots of paper colour. We don't see many 4-way scrollers either.Most of the puzzles are well designed, and the GUI is intuitive. As always in adventure games, I got stuck. However, when I finally discovered

x exactly what happens when you mix a rubber chicken with a nuclear reactor, I laughed at the result.to date, Escape from Monjas is Rastersoft's only published Speccy game. Let's hope it doesn't stay that way.

We seem to have less free time these days, and our attention spans are so wrecked by the internet that  we're probably less likely to read an instruction manual than we were at school! So I'm a sucker for a bit of uncomplicated fun, and Forg Want Love is certainly that.It's a riff on Nifty Lifty, with the main protagonist being a cute frog, and the maurauding lifts are cats. It also develops the level layouts further and gets considerably harder.One talking point is the crazy AY music! It's a sanity testing merry-go-round of chaotic bum notes. The author informs me that it's a cover of Senbonzakura by Hatsune Miku. If Goldfinger did want to make Mr Bond talk, he would do better to play him this for 5 minutes rather than aim a laser beam at his goolies. So were James Bond to play this, he would be wise to play it in 48K mode.Two keys. Run to the heart. Come back. Pause if you need to. Sometimes that's just what the doctor ordered.

GOOD LUCK, BRAVE HERO

These days, game designers and competitions offering highly incentivising cash prizes have given us a wealth of very polished games that would have made us froth at the mouth in disbelief thirty years ago. And of course there's nothing wrong with *that*, but it does mean we see fewer games that embody the 'spirit of the Spectrum' - games that would sit quite happily among some of the best of 1982 (or now), and still have a few clunky features, or aren't afraid to use UDGs. The Golden Fleece is such a game and I instantly loved it.

It's an adventure RPG game, in which your quest is to win the fabled Golden Fleece by sailing around fighting mythical creatures. To have a chance at defeating the monster on the northern-most island, you need to increase your powers by killing off the weaker ones first.The fight sequences are superb, and possibly use a unique mechanic (no-one on the SC forum had seen it before). It combines choices such as advance, attack, evade, and also mixes a dice throw with a ladder that defines fight parameters as you climb it. The ultimate goal is to cleave the monster's head off for a quick win. That is, assuming your monster only has the one head...

The fights are the main event, but there's a lot of fun to be found in exploring the map. You need to hug coastlines as much as possible to avoid the underwater threat of Cetus.Saucerbrain has done his research on mythology, so you might even find that you learn a bit. Not much use in real life, but it might get you a few points on University Challenge.So - the Golden Fleece gets the official "Spirit of the Spectrum" award! Great oldschool fun that reminds us why we stick with this quirky little computer.Oh, and be sure to wait for the RND seed to generate at the start, after all - there's nowt worse than a non-random Aegean.
